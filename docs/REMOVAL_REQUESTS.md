# Removal requests

This corpus contains derived data from the enacted law of sovereign Tribal Nations. Public
availability is not consent to redistribution. A Nation whose material appears here may have it
removed.

## Who may request removal

Any person authorized to act for the Nation: an elected official, a tribal attorney or
attorney general's office, a tribal court, a records or IT office, or anyone the Nation
designates. We do not require proof of authority beyond a request that comes from a Nation's
own contact channel, and we do not require a reason.

## What to send

The name of the Nation, and whether the request covers all of its material or a specific
document. Nothing further is needed. While this release is anonymized for peer review,
requests go through an issue on the repository. A durable contact address replaces that
channel in the de-anonymized release, and this document is updated with it at that point.

## What happens

1. Acknowledgement within seven days.
2. All provisions attributed to that Nation removed from the distributed corpus, and the
   release reissued with a note that a removal occurred. The note names no Nation unless the
   Nation asks that it be named.
3. Any derived file that would allow the removed text to be reconstructed is removed with it.
4. Requests are not negotiated and no justification is requested.

## Reproducibility

Removal takes precedence over reproducibility. Results computed from a version that included
the removed material will be reported as no longer reproducible from the public release. We
will not decline or delay a request in order to keep published numbers reproducible, and we
will not maintain a private copy for that purpose.

## What this procedure does not do

It does not constitute the consent that should have preceded collection. No Tribal government
was consulted before this corpus was built. A removal procedure is a partial remedy offered
after the fact, not a substitute for Authority to control under the CARE Principles.
